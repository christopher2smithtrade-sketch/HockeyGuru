PRESEASON DRY RUN -- exhibition slate. Lines and goalies are guesses, half the rosters are prospects. This is the pipeline warming up; the real board starts on opening night.

The headline is the chance a skater scores at least one goal tonight. It is built bottom-up: projected 5v5 and power-play minutes × his shot rate in each × a finishing rate (what he has actually buried over two seasons, shrunk toward what his shot quality says he should convert), then adjusted for how many shots and chances the opponent gives up, how bad its penalty kill is, how often it takes penalties, who is in the opposing net, what Vegas expects the team to score, home ice and back-to-backs. Overtime, 4-on-4, 5-on-3 and empty-net goals ride on top as a league-average share.

The SOG floor is the same volume projection on its own -- the most predictable stat in hockey and worth 1.5 DK points a shot plus a bonus at five. The composite number on the right is goal% × 100 nudged by the floor; the letter grades are fixed cut-offs, not "top 5% of tonight".

Goalies are graded on projected DraftKings points: win probability from the moneyline, saves from the opponent's shot volume, goals against anchored half to the implied total. Status comes from DailyFaceoff beat-writer confirmations; without one we project the #1 by starts unless he played last night.

The scorecard at the bottom is the honesty check: every board is logged at puck drop and graded the next morning against the box scores. If the A+ rows do not score more often than the B rows, the model is wrong and the anchors move.

Data: NHL API (schedule, rosters, game logs, box scores), MoneyPuck (expected goals, line combos), ESPN (injuries), DailyFaceoff (starting goalies), The Odds API (DraftKings totals and moneylines).
